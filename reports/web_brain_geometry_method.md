# Folded brain geometry and AAL3 mapping

## What changed

The former browser asset `aal3_regions.json` was generated from exposed faces of 2 mm AAL3 voxels. It looked like a stepped/block-like mask. It is **legacy audit material only** and is no longer requested by the application, generated by `prepare_web_data.py`, or accepted by the active validators. The active asset is `anatomical_brain.json` (schema 2.0.0), built by `scripts/build_anatomical_brain.py`.

## Sources and projection

- Anatomical cortex: Nilearn's FreeSurfer **fsaverage6 pial** surfaces, 40,962 vertices per hemisphere (81,924 total; 163,840 triangles). These are real reconstructed folded cortical surfaces, not an AAL mask or a procedurally smoothed blob. Nilearn downloads the source surface from its documented fsaverage dataset when rebuilding geometry.
- Atlas identity: the same `AAL3v1.nii.gz` used for Stage 2 regional expression and the same retained 138 integer label IDs in `data/processed/region_metadata.csv`.
- Cortical atlas projection: the official neuromaps 0.0.7 MNI152-to-fsaverage **registration-fusion** coordinates at 41k density. The two project-stable `data/atlases/regfusion_fsaverage6/*.txt` coordinate files are taken unchanged from the official neuromaps OSF archive. For each surface vertex, the inverse atlas affine transforms the fusion MNI coordinate into the original 2 mm image; nearest-neighbor sampling preserves integer label identity. A triangle is colored/pickable only when two or three projected vertex labels agree on a retained cortical ID. Three-way conflicts and background remain anatomical white.
- Other parcels: subcortical and brainstem AAL3 labels are each extracted as a separate visual isosurface after 1 mm occupancy interpolation and light filtering. This smoothing is **visual only**. The 2 mm source image and integer ID remain the analytical authority. Cortical labels with fewer than 12 projected pial triangles also use this fallback.
- Join rule: every mesh has exactly one original AAL3 `region_id`, and the browser joins stored numerical records by that ID. The browser never changes scores, draws permutations, or computes significance.

In this release, 78 region meshes use projected pial triangles and 60 use label isosurfaces. Of 163,840 anatomy triangles, 138,040 carry retained cortical labels and 25,800 are unassigned; the latter remain visible in neutral anatomy. All 138 retained analytical regions have nonempty indexed meshes. The generated web JSON is approximately 21 MiB uncompressed.

## Important coordinate and boundary caveats

The fsaverage cortical mesh is a surface-template geometry, whereas AAL3 is a 2 mm MNI volume. Registration fusion supplies **label correspondence**, not a perfect coordinate-space registration of the pial mesh to every MNI subcortical surface. Cortex and subcortex are combined as an approximate reference visualization; the statistical join is exact by AAL3 ID, but the depicted parcel outlines are approximate. In particular, 2 mm nearest-neighbor atlas borders can still look saw-toothed on a fine mesh. We do not smooth those colors across original labels or claim that the colored boundary is a precise cortical segmentation.

The browser swaps RAS axes `(x, y, z)` to WebGL `(x, z, y)` so superior is up. Left and right cameras follow negative and positive X, respectively. Region-search focus uses the actual displayed mesh bounds rather than assuming the volumetric MNI centroid coincides with the fsaverage pial coordinate.

## Modes and checks

- **Anatomy only:** opaque neutral pial geometry with no research coloring.
- **Research:** translucent value overlay on folded cortex, neutral unassigned anatomy, and discrete subcortical/brainstem meshes.
- **Atlas validation:** `/dev/atlas-validation` or the legacy `?mode=atlas` URL; deterministic ID colors and a clickable 138-region list. The ID colors are diagnostic and do not represent disease values.

Run `python scripts/build_anatomical_brain.py`, `python scripts/validate_brain_region_mapping.py`, and `python scripts/validate_web_data.py`. The latter two fail on duplicate/missing IDs, bad indices, nonfinite geometry, canonical/deployed mismatch, and numerical disagreement with the frozen Stage 6 Parkinson table. Browser screenshot tests show anatomy-only, real Parkinson data, and atlas-ID mapping. No scientific analysis inputs were edited for this redesign.
